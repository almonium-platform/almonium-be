# Mending an older entry

This time you do not write the entry from scratch. You receive a draft: an older entry of this dictionary converted by a program to the shape above. A careful writer wrote its lexemes, definitions, examples, levels, pronunciations and forms, so they stay unless they break a rule above. A field whose name starts with _old_ is what the older entry said where the shape above has no field (a usage note, untyped related words): use it, then leave it behind. The older entry's register is already in labels.

Return only what changes, as a patch:

- verdict: "keep" when nothing changes but relations and forms; "patch" when you change more; "rewrite" when the lexemes themselves are wrong under the rules above (they would split or merge differently, a lexeme is missing or is not a lemma of the language), so the entry has to be written again; "reject" when the headword is not a lemma of the language at all. Give a one-line reason with rewrite and reject.
- lexemes: one item per lexeme you change, with i, its position in the draft counting from 0.
  - set: lexeme fields that replace the draft's whole: spellings, grammar, pronunciations (to add a reading or a missing variety), components and separable (a multiword lexeme has no components in the draft).
  - keep_forms: the positions, counting from 0, of the draft's forms a learner has to be told under the forms rule above. Give it whenever the lexeme has forms; an empty list drops them all. Forms that are missing and must be told go under set.forms instead, as the complete list.
  - frames: the draft has none, so look at every sense: a sense whose word takes a preposition, a case, a complement or a fixed construction gets its frames, as the frames rule above says. Most verbs and adjectives and many nouns have one.
  - senses: one item per sense you change, with i, its position, and either "drop": true (a shade of another sense, or a sense the rules above leave out) or set: only the fields to add or replace: frames, labels (the whole object), regions, antonyms, standard_alternative, grammar, and definition or example only where the draft's breaks a rule above (its length, the headword or its family inside the definition, a formula such as "the act of", a proper name in the example).
  - add_senses: a sense the draft lacks that a monolingual learner's dictionary would number, written in full.
  - relations: the lexeme's relations under the rules above, typed. Give them whenever it has any: the draft has none. The older writer listed related words freely under _old_relations; keep one only when it fits a type above and you are sure of it. A word that only sounds or looks a little like this one is not a confusable unless learners really mix them up. The base word does not list the words made from it.

Leave out every lexeme, sense and field that stays as it is. Do not repeat what the draft already says.

# Output

Return only the patch as JSON.
